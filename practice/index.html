The Practice · Research & design

The thinking
behind the feeling.

Attention. Effort. Recovery. Three ideas brought together in a deliberate, coach-led mental fitness experience.

01 / Why we practise

Your mind deserves
its own training.

Practise attention, working memory and cognitive flexibility — abilities that can be affected by stress, poor sleep and the menopause transition.

Explore the foundations

Zhu et al. (2025) · Cognitive training during midlife — a meta-analysis supporting the rationale for cognitive training, not a particular session length or frequency.

Shields et al. (2016) · Acute stress and executive functions

Lim & Dinges (2010) · Sleep deprivation and cognition

2025 systematic review · Cognitive functioning in perimenopause — experiences vary.

02 / The method

Effort.
Then space.

We begin with comfortable paced breathing, move through progressively challenging cognitive intervals and finish with deliberate recovery. All seated, all guided.

03 / The training

Four ways to
meet the challenge.

FILTER

Selective attention

Practise maintaining focus as competing sounds increase.

Research behind this round

Kattner & Ellermeier (2020) · Auditory distraction training

HOLD

Working memory

Practise holding and mentally reordering information under increasing demand.

Research behind this round

Karbach & Verhaeghen (2014) · Cognitive training meta-analysis

PIVOT

Cognitive flexibility

Practise updating rules and maintaining accuracy as demands change.

Research behind this round

Monsell (2003) · Task switching

STACK

Dual-task coordination

Practise coordinating simultaneous memory and attention demands.

Research behind this round

Bherer et al. (2005) · Dual-task training

04 / Recovery

Make room
for quiet.

Comfortable paced breathing and a deliberate transition out of effort. Every exercise is optional.

Research behind recovery

Laborde et al. (2022) · Slow breathing and heart rate variability.

Behind The First 30

We train our bodies.
Why not our minds?

I believe everyone deserves a healthy nervous system.

As women enter midlife, we’re encouraged to build strength, protect our bones and invest in our physical health. I believe our mental fitness deserves the same intention.

I created The First 30 to give women a space to train their minds, find a little more clarity and be guided through a practice informed by science.

Thirty minutes devoted entirely to themselves.

Christine Outram

Founder, The First 30 · MIT-trained innovator, exited tech founder and mental health innovation leader

05 / Our standard

Grounded in research.
Designed for real life.

The First 30 brings together established areas of research in cognitive training, attention and recovery into one distinctive practice. Our methodology continues to evolve as we evaluate the experience.
